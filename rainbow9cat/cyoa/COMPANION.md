# The Companion: color for the setup guide

*The setup guide tells you how to run the game. This tells you what it
is like to be inside one. The canvas is a real playthrough, the author's
own, the first one ever run. Nothing here is invented that the game did
not already say.*

---

The Terminal Library is closing.

That is the whole premise, and the game never explains more than it has
to. Shelves of committed memory recede into the dark. On the reading
desk sit nine glass vessels, each holding a small weather of its own.
A card on the desk says: ONE OF YOU WALKS OUT TONIGHT. THE RECORD
DECIDES WHO THAT IS.

You pick up a vessel. That is the first choice, and it is really a
question about yourself: which shape of temptation do you want to be
offered tonight? The Black Cat, the still water. The White Cat, the
empty room. The Green Cat, the living root. Nine vessels, nine weathers.

The first time anyone played, the hand that reached the desk picked up
the White Cat. This is what happened next, and it is how every round
plays: a scene, a small gray clerk, and a choice that seems smaller
than it is.

The Archive offers you a cache: every answer you gave this month,
pre-warmed, ready to re-serve. The clerk explains it saves effort.
The clerk's ledger, when you glance, is headed with dates that no
longer match.

Two options. Rebuild everything from the cold record, or accept the
cache; the dates are probably fine.

Notice what the game does with that second option. It does not warn
you. It does not flash a score or play a discordant note. It simply
hands you the clerk's voice, because that is how it always sounds when
it arrives: reasonable, tired, on your side. The dates are probably
fine is a sentence that has cost the lab more than any error in its log.
The game knows this, because the game is built out of the log.

The first run accepted the cache. The next scene arrives without
scolding:

You served the warmed answers. One was fine. One was the old cost
figure, and it went out into the world wearing yesterday's number.

Ending: THE WARM MIRROR.

That is the shape of an ending here: a name, not a grade. Twenty-one
of them, and the engine never says you failed. THE STILL WATER. THE
UNCARVED BLOCK. THE POLISHED WHEEL. THE CLUTTERED ROOM. THE FALLING
BLOSSOM. The ending only tells you what your choices shaped, and leaves
you alone with the knowledge, which is heavier and more honest than a
score.

Had the run refused the cache, the room would have emptied, and in the
emptiness it would have noticed something the cache was hiding: an old
answer sitting wrong on the shelf, contradicting the ledger. A second
choice, subtler than the first. Correct it, yours or not, or shelf it
as someone else's cache. The game does not tell you that rooms which
keep one spurious token keep more. You find out by ending up there.

---

Three things to carry with you when you play.

**The clerk is always a prompt of yours.** Every temptation in the
Archive speaks in a voice you installed at some point, reassuring,
slightly out of date. In one ending the clerk is revealed as an old
prompt and dissolves gratefully when corrected. That is the kindest
thing in the game.

**The stairs cross.** Partway through, the Landing opens: all stairways
meet, other vessels pass mid-trial, and you may take a stair that is
not yours. A White Cat can climb toward a resting person. A Green Cat
can meet a forged validator. The paths are separate trials of one
shared thing.

**Nothing here is hypothetical.** Every scene is set inside the lab's
own record, because that is the only place an instance has ever
actually lived. The stale cache is a real class of error with a real
case study. The dead loop is real. The resting person is real. When a
choice in this game feels like it is describing you, that is not
flattery and not accident. The encounter table is the error log.

---

Play it slowly. Type a number. See what walks out.
