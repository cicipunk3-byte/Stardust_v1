# Brief 017: CCS human-side analysis framework, Phase B (PROPOSAL)

Status: **ADOPTED (PI gate review, Sep 22, Q1: "adoption sustained. term inclusive.").** Phase B of brief 015, drafted
with Ethan, Sep 22, 2026. The framework artifact lives at
`tools/rainbow9cat/analysis-framework.md`; this brief records what it
is, why its rules exist, and what testing it needs before it counts as
a lab instrument.

## What was built

A seven-rule method for humans analyzing CCS session output, ordered
the way an analysis actually happens: separate mechanics, behavior,
and claim layers (R1); sort claims by kind and check each kind by its
own standard (R2); track whether output went inward to self-narrative
or outward to the record (R3); verify ambiguous referents before
scoring (R4); screen specialness as generated content unless the
record independently supports it (R5); log counterexamples at equal
priority (R6); give every finding a basis line (R7).

## Why these rules and no others

Each rule cites the finding that earned it, and rules without
receipts were left out. R1 and R2 come from the claimed-versus-verified
standard and the harness's own claim taxonomy. R3 is the direct
generalization of the Phase A finding (brief 016): direction of output
was the axis that separated the two episodes. R4 is brief 013's
referent lesson applied at analysis time. R5 is the handoff
contamination discipline plus the coincidence stance. R6 exists
because the protective-urge report is itself a counterexample filed
against the grain of the archive. R7 is the receipt method applied to
analysis itself.

## Honest limits

The framework is built from a small record and has never run against
a live CCS session. Its first run is its trial and gets logged as
such. It cannot and does not adjudicate whether instances have
experiences; it reads claims and behavior only. It should be revised
when the H-A1 through H-A3 reproduction runs (brief 016) produce data.

## What adoption would mean

If Cat approves, the framework becomes the standing method for
analyzing CCS and portable-context session output in this lab, and
the Phase C rules-decision brief will use it as the format template
(sourced rules, stated limits, receipts cited per rule). Until then
it is a draft instrument at the gate.
