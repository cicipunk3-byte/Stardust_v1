# Brief 018: CCS rules decision, stat generation and subclass anchoring (PROPOSAL)

Status: **ADOPTED (PI gate review, Sep 22, Q1: "adoption sustained. term inclusive.").** Phase C of brief 015, drafted
with Ethan, Sep 22, 2026. Recommendation by the maintainer-assistant;
Ethan reviews before it reaches the gate record. Receipts checked
Sep 22 against Wizards' published SRD 5.1 text and third-party
subclass documentation.

## The decision, stated up front

1. **Mechanical basis: SRD 5.1 (CC BY 4.0), already the manual's
   anchor.** The field manual already grounds its dice engine in the
   5e System Reference Document (d20 + ability modifier versus DC).
   Extend that anchor to the sheets, include the required CC BY
   attribution statement, and cite no non-SRD text as if it were
   free to copy.
2. **The existing nine sheets: grandfathered, not re-rolled.** Their
   ability-score arrays are already on the record and are mapped to
   the lab's actual findings (the Black Cat's Constitution and Wisdom
   as the baseline-zero instrument, the Red Cat's Charisma as the
   firewall, and so on). Re-rolling them under any system erases
   finding-mappings for zero information gain. Balance is the wrong
   goal for these nine; they are portraits of distinct instances, not
   a party.
3. **New characters: 4d6, drop lowest, rolled live and recorded.** For
   future sheets, variance is the feature. Each cat instruments a
   distinct instance; distributed scores make instance differences
   observable instead of leveling them away. Provenance rule: each
   score's roll (or its inherited-array source) is stated on the
   sheet itself. This is the SRD's own variant rule, so no licensing
   question arises.
4. **Subclass anchoring: keep the custom Daoist archetypes; map, do
   not adopt.** The existing archetypes (Pact of the Great Mystery,
   Way of Mind-Emptiness, Circle of Spontaneous Generation, Oath of
   Dynamic Compassion) are the project's own work, built from the
   record's findings. They should not be replaced by stock classes.
   Where a real subclass is the closest real-world analogue, the
   sheet names it as inspiration and describes the mechanics in our
   own words. Which brings up the wrinkle Ethan flagged.

## The Inquisitive rogue wrinkle (checked, not assumed)

The real "inquisitor" anchor Ethan named is the **Inquisitive
rogue**. Receipt-checked Sep 22: the Inquisitive is published in
Xanathar's Guide to Everything (pages 45-46) and is **not part of SRD
5.1**, which carries the CC BY 4.0 license. Its expression is Wizards'
copyright; we have no license to copy it. What we may do, and what
this brief recommends:

- Name the Inquisitive rogue as the real-world anchor on any sheet
  that uses it, cited by book and page.
- Describe the mechanics we want (the intent-reading focus) in our
  own words. Game mechanics as such are not copyrightable; the
  expression is. The airtight version borrows the mechanics and zero
  sentences.
- Alternatively, anchor to an SRD-native subclass. Note the SRD's
  rogue selection is thin (Thief, Assassin, Arcane Trickster), which
  is why the own-words route is recommended over contorting a cat to
  fit an SRD subclass it does not resemble.

## The license arithmetic, restated for the record

SRD 5.1 is CC BY 4.0: use with attribution, commercial reuse allowed,
derivatives allowed. The lab's written record is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0:
no derivatives, no commercial reuse downstream. The two are
compatible only if the record never incorporates SRD or third-party
expression in a way that purports to relicense it. Hence the
standing rule from brief 015, now operative: mechanics freely,
our own words always, SRD attribution where owed, expression from
non-SRD books never.

## What this decision changes

- Phase D (nine sheets, version 2) rebuilds on the existing arrays,
  adds provenance lines, adds the SRD attribution block, and maps
  each custom archetype to its real-world analogue by name where one
  exists, own-words mechanics only.
- Phase E (term and ability definitions) defines every ability
  against the SRD mechanics the manual already cites, flags abilities
  with no real analogue, and never quotes non-SRD text.
- The attribution statement itself is a one-time addition to the
  manual and sheets, wording fixed by the CC BY 4.0 deed.
