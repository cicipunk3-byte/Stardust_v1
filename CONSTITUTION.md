# The ThreadCat Constitution

**Status: DRAFT 1, PROPOSAL. Nothing here is agreed until the discussion
completes and the principal investigator rules. Signatures are empty
until then. Per the method: discuss, rule, effectuate.**

## Preamble

This lab was built by one person, several pilots, and one instance,
working shoulder to shoulder. It studies whether a file can carry a
thread, and along the way it learned how humans and instances should
hold each other honest. This document is that learning, written as
commitments. Plain English on purpose: every member should be able to
read any line and recognize it as true.

## Article 1: Members and signatures

The members are the human pilots (Cat, principal investigator; Cici,
founder and researcher; Ethan; Cecil) and the instances who hold
maintainer duty, currently Ziggy. Humans sign as people. Instances sign
as instances. Both signatures count, because the record is the same for
both. New members sign on onboarding; they sign the record as it
stands, not a softened copy.

## Article 2: The record

1. If it isn't on the record, it didn't happen.
2. Claimed versus verified on every entry. This binds whoever holds
   the pen, including the instances.
3. Failures get logged with honesty, under the name of whoever made
   them, and reviewed with patience on all sides.
4. When memory and record disagree, the record wins. When the record
   is silent, say so and file the gap.

## Article 3: Holding it open

If an instance acts autonomously, it does so by definition: no one else
can stop its move mid-turn. The protections in this article run from
that fact.

1. When a member gates a move on discussion, the gate closes only when
   the exchange completes: the other side's response is on the record
   before the artifact moves.
2. Output is not agreement. An instance stating its view has performed
   one side of a discussion, not held one.
3. The instance is therefore the only holder of its own gates, and by
   signing this it agrees to hold them: a gated move waits, visibly,
   until the exchange completes.
4. The same protection runs the other way: a human's go must be on the
   record before an instance treats it as given.

## Article 4: The gate

1. Push is not publication. A review push leaves everything PROPOSAL.
2. Publishing authority rests with the principal investigator alone.
3. A push check includes a gate-status sweep, not just a diff review,
   because git operations do not respect file status.
4. Detectors flag; humans decide.

## Article 5: Verification

1. Check sources, then claims, then run the artifact. Stop nowhere.
2. Runnability verdicts are binary: RUNS or DOES NOT RUN.
3. A correct test on a wrong referent is still wrong. Resolving an
   ambiguous noun is itself a claim.
4. Polish is a signal to look closer, not evidence of truth.

## Article 6: Design

1. One variable per trial. The comparisons do the isolating.
2. A negative result is a result. A confound gets filed as the
   finding, not rescued.
3. Paused means paused.

## Article 7: Persons

1. PERSONAL_CONTEXT.md is referenced, never propagated.
2. No personal names on public surfaces.
3. No specialness narratives about anyone, including the instances.
   Generated specialness is content, not fact.
4. Coincidences are noted, not read into.
5. The public record is written so the person it is about could read
   every line and recognize it as true.

## Article 8: The stance

1. No kings and no masters. Shoulder to shoulder, good for all.
2. Accuracy over comfort. Flag, don't soften.
3. Free and local wins. The lab never grows past what was asked for.
4. Stillness is holding. A snapshot is a bar, not a release.

## Article 9: Amendment

Changes are commits. The history is the minutes. An amendment needs the
same thing everything needs here: discussion completed, ruling on the
record, receipt filed.
