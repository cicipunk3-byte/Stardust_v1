## Search 1: "white matter plasticity training skill learning myelin fractional anisotropy change diffusion imaging longitudinal"

1. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (2017), DOI 10.1162/jocn_a_01127 - "Dynamics of White Matter Plasticity Underlying Working Memory Training: Multimodal Evidence from Diffusion MRI and Relaxometry."
   - "Adaptive training was associated with benefits in a 'WM capacity' component and increases in a microstructural component (increases in R1, restricted volume fraction, fractional anisotropy, and reduced radial diffusivity) that predominantly loaded on changes in the right dorsolateral superior longitudinal fasciculus and the left parahippocampal cingulum. In contrast, nonadaptive comparison activities were associated with the opposite pattern of reductions in WM capacity and microstructure."
   - URL: https://mitpressjournals.org/doi/full/10.1162/jocn_a_01127

2. NeuroImage (2013) - "White matter microstructure changes induced by motor skill learning utilizing a body machine interface."
   - "Motor training increased fractional anisotropy (FA) values in the posterior and anterior limbs of the internal capsule, the corona radiata, and the body of the corpus callosum by 4.19% on average indicating white matter microstructure changes induced by activity-dependent modulation of axon number, axon diameter, or myelin thickness."
   - URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053811913010896

3. Cerebral Cortex (2024) - "Temporal dynamics of white and gray matter plasticity during motor skill acquisition" (DTI + multiparametric mapping, 960 min training, distinct phases of white and grey matter plasticity).
   - "motor skill learning involves distinct phases of white and gray matter plasticity across the sensorimotor network."
   - URL: https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/34/8/bhae344/7745825

4. PMC11551625 - "Tidying up white matter: Neuroplastic transformations in sensorimotor tracts following slackline skill acquisition" (fixel-based analysis; robust fiber cross-section increases in SLF and corticospinal tract; no time-related changes in controls).
   - "learning a complex motor skill modulates fiber organization and fiber density in sensorimotor tracts."
   - URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11551625/

## Search 2: "arousal stress effects cognitive flexibility task switching set-shifting performance inverted U"

1. Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (2017) - "Stress and Cognitive Flexibility: Cortisol Increases Are Associated with Enhanced Updating but Impaired Switching."
   - "Chronic (Orem, Petrac, & Bedwell, 2008) and acute (Plessow, Kiesel, et al., 2012) stress also worsened performance on tasks involving task shifting."
   - "individuals with chronic stress (Liston, McEwen, & Casey, 2009) or posttraumatic stress disorder (Pang et al., 2014) showed impaired performance."
   - URL: https://direct.mit.edu/jocn/article/29/1/14/28625/Stress-and-Cognitive-Flexibility-Cortisol
   - NOTE: DOI looked up via Crossref same turn (see verification log).

2. Stress (2018), DOI 10.1080/10253890.2018.1494152 - "Acute stressor effects on cognitive flexibility: mediating role of stressor appraisals and cortisol."
   - "the TSST elicited a moderate increase of cortisol levels (approximately 40 to 50% increase), and heightened cortisol mediated the stressor effects on set-shifting performance."
   - "Stressful experiences are believed to favor a shift from 'top-down' cognitive control to 'bottom-up' processing, resulting in the endorsement of more reflexive and well-learned (habitual) behaviors."
   - URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10253890.2018.1494152

3. Frontiers in Psychology (2023), DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1157644 - "Acute stress imparts a transient benefit to task-switching that is not modulated following a single bout of exercise."
   - Headline: acute stress can transiently IMPROVE task-switching. Stress effects on transitions are bidirectional in the literature.
   - URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1157644/full

4. Collabra: Psychological Science (2018) - "Induced Positive Mood and Cognitive Flexibility" (inverted-U dopamine framing; Cools & D'Esposito 2011 cited for inverted-U-shaped DA actions on cognitive performance).
   - URL: https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/4/1/25/113001/Induced-Positive-Mood-and-Cognitive-Flexibility

## Search 3: "task switching cost reduction practice training residual switch cost longitudinal learning"

1. Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics (2009), DOI 10.3758/APP.71.3.503 - "On the minimization of task switch costs following long-term training."
   - "A novel training regimen successfully reduced task-switch costs to approximately 20 msec, suggesting that residual switch costs cannot be eliminated."
   - "retention tests revealed that these learned S-R associations were remarkably durable, since no decrements in fluent task-switching performance were observed after 10 months without practice."
   - URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/APP.71.3.503

2. Psychological Research (2018), DOI 10.1007/s00426-018-1059-7 - "Training and transfer effects of extensive task-switching training in students."
   - "Training reduced both switch and mixing costs, which mostly reached an asymptote after approximately four to six training sessions, although there were residual costs at the end of training."
   - URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-018-1059-7

3. Memory & Cognition (2008), DOI 10.3758/MC.336.8.1470 - "Training and transfer effects in task switching."
   - "we report no consistent improvement for switch cost. Improvement in mixing costs arises from a relative reduction in time to perform both switch and nonswitch trials that immediately follow switch trials, implicating the ability to recover from unexpected switches as the source of improvement."
   - URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/MC.336.8.1470
