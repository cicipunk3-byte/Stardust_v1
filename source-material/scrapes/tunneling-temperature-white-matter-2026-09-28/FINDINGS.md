## Finding 1: The tunneling-temperature relationship is textbook, measured physics

- Escape from a metastable state happens by two channels: quantum tunneling through the barrier and thermal activation over it ([Phys Rev B / arXiv cond-mat/9805071](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9805071)).
- There is a **crossover temperature Tc**. Above it, thermal jumps dominate; below it, tunneling; and the crossover can be abrupt (first-order-like) or smooth, with the smooth case called **thermally assisted tunneling** ([Faraday Discussions 2016, 10.1039/C6FD00136J](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2016/fd/c6fd00136j)).
- Affleck's semiclassical formalism interpolates the whole range in one expression ([arXiv 2609.21625](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21625)). This is the same Affleck already cited on Level I of the staircase paper.
- **It is experimentally measured, not just theoretical:** crossover temperatures and jump rates computed for an Mn4 molecular magnet agree "in excellent agreement with previously reported experimental measurements" (Faraday Discussions 2016, above). The signature is an Arrhenius plot: log(rate) vs 1/T is a straight line in the thermal regime and flattens at low temperature where tunneling takes over.

**What this means for the theory:** Γ_th = ν·exp(−ΔE/T) has T in it explicitly. If the effective temperature of the mental-state system (arousal, metabolic heat, sympathetic tone) rises, the thermal channel's rate rises exponentially while the tunneling channel is roughly temperature-insensitive. **Temperature selects the channel.** A testable corollary: transitions that occur at high arousal should be predominantly thermal-channel (effort-like), and the channel balance should shift with measured temperature.

## Finding 2: White matter is the warmest brain tissue, and its conduction is temperature-sensitive at sub-degree scales

- The hottest parts of the brain are the deep cerebral structures: internal capsule, basal ganglia, and subcortical white matter of the corona radiata, 0.5 to 0.6°C warmer than blood ([Frontiers in Neuroscience 2014 review, 10.3389/fnins.2014.00307](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2014.00307/full)).
- In humans, the thalamus runs 1.64°C hotter than cortical grey matter, and "white matter-predominating areas were relatively warm" ([Brain 2022, 10.1093/brain/awab466](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/145/6/2031/6604351)). Whole-brain temperature follows a daily rhythm (the "daily heatwave"), rising and falling with sleep and arousal (Frontiers 2014; [PMC7614663](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7614663)).
- **The Uhthoff phenomenon is the proof that small temperature shifts matter in white matter:** a rise of ~0.5°C closes voltage-gated sodium channels in demyelinated axons, producing conduction slowing and even complete conduction block, reversible with cooling ([StatPearls NBK470244](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK470244/)). Demyelinated axons block at lower temperatures than healthy ones ([EJAP 2025, 10.1007/s00421-025-05838-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-025-05838-7)). 60 to 87% of MS patients report heat sensitivity, and the trigger list includes psychological stress (StatPearls; Handbook chapter 2018).

**What this means for the theory:** v0's claim was that white matter constrains the barrier term Δ_ij (connectome/FA structure). The new claim on top: the barrier-bearing substrate is also the thermally most active tissue, and its signal-carrying integrity is measurably modulated by shifts smaller than the daily brain-temperature swing. In the theory's language, T_eff is not an outside variable; it is coupled to the substrate that defines the landscape. One number (temperature) touches both the rate (channel selection) and the terrain (conduction in the barrier tissue).

## Finding 3: What is measurable and provable, stated separately

**Established and provable today (Level I / Level II physics and physiology):**
- Crossover temperature Tc exists and is experimentally measurable in physical systems (Faraday Discussions 2016).
- Arrhenius scaling of thermally activated rates; the Arrhenius plot as the diagnostic.
- Uhthoff conduction block: magnitude (~0.5°C threshold), mechanism (Na+ channel closure), reversibility (cooling).
- Human brain temperature gradients (up to ~2.4°C spatial range within one brain at one time) and their daily rhythm.
- Peripheral skin temperature as a sympathetic-arousal readout (previous scrape: mood rings, EDA).

**Measurable but unmeasured (the conjecture's exposure, unchanged in kind):**
- Whether identity-state (lane) transitions in a specific system show any crossover-like signature at all.
- Whether pre-transition peripheral temperature drift exists above chance (the ring observation).
- d_FS at case scale; Γ_q remains not behaviorally isolable.

## Rivals and holes (updated)

1. **Arousal-covariate rival (new, strongest):** everything H-ring and H-photo can detect is also explained by ordinary stress fluctuation with no transitions involved. The discriminating quantity is transition-locked timing, not level. Any protocol must beat "the ring just tracks stress."
2. The instrument limits from the previous scrapes stand: mood rings read mean temperature and miss the ~0.04°C vasomotion rhythm; aura cameras map EDA through a fiction table.
3. Uhthoff is a demyelination phenomenon; extending conduction-temperature logic to healthy white matter at sub-degree scale is an extrapolation, stated as such.
4. Peripheral temperature is a proxy for arousal/T_eff, not a direct measurement of any brain temperature. The central-peripheral coupling is itself literature (arousal-linked brain temperature shifts) but is not measured in this system.
5. n=1, observational anchor is untimestamped folk report, confounds uncontrolled.
