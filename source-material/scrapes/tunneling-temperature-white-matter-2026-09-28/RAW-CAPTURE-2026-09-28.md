## Search 1: "quantum tunneling versus thermal activation crossover temperature rate escape transition temperature physics"

1. arXiv:cond-mat/9805071v2 (Phys Rev B 57, 13639, 1998)  -  "Quantum-classical transition of the escape rate of a uniaxial spin system in an arbitrarily directed field."
   - "The crossover boundaries correspond to the argument of the erfc function in Eq. (3.43) taking the values ±1. to the nonvanishing quantum transparency of the barrier. At zero temperature the escape rate is higher because tunneling occurs from the ground-state level which is slightly above the bottom of the potential."
   - "Below the crossover temperature ... Such a regime is called thermally assisted tunneling (TAT)."
   - "The two fundamental mechanisms of the escape of a particle from a metastable potential well are quantum tunneling through the barrier and the classical thermal activation over the barrier."
   - URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9805071

2. Faraday Discussions (RSC), DOI:10.1039/C6FD00136J  -  "Classical to quantum mechanical tunneling mechanism crossover in thermal transitions between magnetic states" (2016).
   - "As the temperature decreases below Tc, a second negative eigenvalue of δ2S appears, corresponding to the quantum delocalization. This signals the transition from thermally-activated jumps to quantum tunnelling."
   - "Calculations of the jump rate and crossover temperature for tunneling are also made for a molecular magnet containing an Mn4 group. The results are in excellent agreement with previously reported experimental measurements on this system."
   - "The crossover from jumps to tunneling is in some cases abrupt, as in a first-order phase transition, but in other cases smooth, as in a second-order transition. In the latter the tunneling is thermally assisted."
   - URL: https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2016/fd/c6fd00136j

3. arXiv 2609.21625  -  "Semiclassical Arrhenius law for quantum-thermal escape rates."
   - "Affleck's result is compact and widely used: the escape rate is a Boltzmann average of the Hill-Wheeler outgoing flux through a parabolic barrier over a continuum of energies. It interpolates between thermal escape at high temperature and quantum tunneling below the crossover temperature."
   - "The crossover between thermally-assisted quantum tunneling at low temperatures and quantum-corrected thermal hopping at high temperatures is naturally identified by the crossover temperature."
   - URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21625

4. ScienceDirect S0375960102007223  -  "Crossover from quantum tunneling to classical hopping of barrier transition in nano-magnets" (Physics Letters A, 2002).
   - "The decay of metastable states or transition between degenerate states through potential barrier is dominated at temperature close to zero by quantum tunneling, whereas thermal activation becomes more and more important with increasing temperature, and becomes decisive mechanism when the temperature is over a critical value."
   - URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0375960102007223

## Search 2: "temperature effects white matter axon conduction velocity Uhthoff demyelination brain temperature"

1. Uhthoff Phenomenon  -  StatPearls, NCBI Bookshelf NBK470244 (2022).
   - "Studies have shown a decrease in conduction velocity with increasing temperature in multiple sclerosis patients. The temperature-related slowing of conduction velocity can be reversed with cooling."
   - "Temperature-sensitive conduction blockade of partially demyelinated axons in the demyelinated plaques is the most widely accepted mechanism."
   - "Hence, even a small increase in temperature (eg, 0.5°C) in individuals with multiple sclerosis results in the closure of voltage-gated sodium channels in demyelinated axons, thereby compromising action potential depolarization and decreasing the safety threshold for high-fidelity nerve transmission. This can produce abnormalities ranging from delayed conduction to complete conduction block."
   - Triggers listed include exercise, fever, hot showers, "psychological stress."
   - URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK470244/

2. iScience (2025), S2589004225017286 / PMC12570372  -  "New insights into the influence of temperature on axonal transport and function in myelinated regions of Schwann cells."
   - "MS patients commonly experience heat sensitivity: even small increases in core body temperature can temporarily worsen their neurological symptoms, a phenomenon known as Uhthoff's phenomenon."
   - "a 2022 survey of 757 MS patients reported that 87% experienced heat sensitivity (either heat-only or heat-and-cold), with 58% being heat-only sensitive and 29% sensitive to both heat and cold."
   - URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12570372/

3. Handbook of Clinical Neurology chapter  -  "Thermoregulatory dysfunction in multiple sclerosis" (2018).
   - "An estimated 60–80% of MS patients experience temporary worsening of clinical signs and neurologic symptoms with heat exposure (Uhthoff's phenomenon)."
   - "gray-matter areas of the brain such as the hypothalamus, medulla, and brainstem are susceptible to demyelination, thereby resulting in impaired control of autonomic and endocrine function."
   - URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/handbook/abs/pii/B9780444640741000422

4. European Journal of Applied Physiology (2025), DOI:10.1007/s00421-025-05838-7  -  "Toward isolating perceptual and physiological contributors to heat sensitivity in multiple sclerosis."
   - "Increased axonal temperature reduces conduction amplitude, leading to conduction block (Eliasson et al. 1986; Gasser 1931; Tasaki & Fujita 1948). Demyelinated axons develop reversible conduction block at lower temperatures than myelinated axons."
   - [Crossref-verified 2025-09-28: title, EJAP, 2025.]
   - URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-025-05838-7

5. J Appl Physiol 2010 special topic  -  demyelination + temperature: "Increased temperature further reduces the safety factor, thereby influencing the threshold of current to excite an axon."
   - URL: https://journals.physiology.org/doi/pdf/10.1152/japplphysiol.00460.2010

## Search 3: "human brain temperature deep white matter warmer than body thermoregulation hypothalamus measurement"

1. Frontiers in Neuroscience (2014), DOI:10.3389/fnins.2014.00307  -  "Brain temperature and its fundamental properties: a review for clinical neuroscientists."
   - "the center of cerebral hemispheres (internal capsule, basal ganglia, and the subcortical white matter in the corona radiata) and the midbrain reticular formation are the hottest parts of the brain, 0.5–0.6°C warmer than the blood (Hayward and Baker, 1969)."
   - "A thermal gradient of 1.4°C between the hypothalamus and the cortex was reported in free moving cats as early as 1938."
   - "(1) an approximately 1°C gradient exists between the cooler cortical regions and warmer basal regions; ... (4) a rise and fall in temperature occurs with sleep and arousal; and (5) a rise of temperature in neuronal pathways is associated with sensory stimuli on an extended time course beyond the duration of the stimulus."
   - URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2014.00307/full

2. Brain (2022), DOI:10.1093/brain/awab466  -  "A daily temperature rhythm in the human brain predicts survival after brain injury."

   - "In the cerebrum, white matter-predominating areas were relatively warm."
   - "The TBr range overall was 36.1 to 40.9°C, whilst the mean maximal spatial TBr range (difference between hottest and coolest voxel in an individual at any given time point) was 2.41 ± 0.46°C."
