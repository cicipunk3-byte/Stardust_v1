## Key hits (with URLs)

### The peer-reviewed core: autonomic measures DO differ across identity states (under provocation)
- Reinders, A. A. T. S. et al. "Psychobiological characteristics of dissociative identity disorder: a symptom provocation study." *Biological Psychiatry* 60(7):730-740 (2006). [PubMed 17008145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17008145/) ; [Groningen portal record](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/psychobiological-characteristics-of-dissociative-identity-disorde/) ; [ScienceDirect S000632230600388X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S000632230600388X). Design: 2x2, two stimulus scripts x two dissociative identity states (NIS "neutral" vs TIS "traumatic"). Verbatim-in-substance: "These identities involve different subjective reactions, cardiovascular responses and cerebral activation patterns to a trauma-related memory script."
- Reinders et al. 2012, "Fact or Factitious? A Psychobiological Study of Authentic and Simulated Dissociative Identity States," [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0039279): simulating controls (high AND low fantasy-prone, motivated) could NOT mimic the psychophysiological and neural reactions of genuine identity states; findings "at odds with" the pure role-play account.
- Schlumpf et al. 2014, "Dissociative Part-Dependent Resting-State Activity in DID: A Controlled fMRI Perfusion Study," [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0098795): resting-state perfusion differs across dissociative parts; not attributable to imagination.
- Reinders et al. 2014, "Opposite brain emotion-regulation patterns in identity states of DID: A PET study," [Eur Neuropsychopharmacol / ScienceDirect S0925492714001218](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925492714001218). CRITICAL honest line, verbatim: **"Psychophysiological biomarkers, including blood pressure, heart rate and skin conductance, were inconclusive."** (While neuroimaging + hormonal markers: oxytocin up, prolactin up, TNF-alpha down, differentiated states.)
- Earlier context: Griffin et al. 1997 (cited in [PMC2526371](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2526371/)): high dissociators show SUPPRESSED autonomic responses (HR, skin conductance) to trauma interview vs low dissociators. [Mirror-confrontation study, European Journal of Psychotraumatology 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20008198.2018.1472991): dissociative disorders show blunted/dissociated psychophysiological stress responses; sympathetic-parasympathetic COACTIVATION hypothesized in dissociative stress; ~30% of PTSD patients are "physiological non-responders" (dissociative subtype).

### Temperature specifically: NOT tracked in any peer-reviewed study found
- Direct searches for body/finger/skin temperature measured across identity switches: no peer-reviewed study found. The only direct hits are community reports ([r/DID thread: "Alters Affecting Body Temperature"](https://www.reddit.com/r/DID/comments/rue8f5/alters_affecting_body_temperature/)): self-reports of feeling cold/shivering with triggering, freeze/flop response framing. Anecdotal, unverified, cited as evidence the phenomenology is widely reported, NOT as evidence of measurement.
- Adjacent validated link: dissociative states differ in autonomic profile (above), and finger temperature indexes sympathetic vasoconstrictor tone (see mood-rings scrape), but the two have never been joined: no study in this scrape measured peripheral temperature across identity-state transitions.
