## Key hits (with URLs)

### Material science
- [Wikipedia: Mood ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_ring): thermochromic (thermotropic) liquid crystals change color with finger temperature; finger temperature (at constant ambient) is significantly determined by peripheral blood flow. Inventors credited: Joshua Reynolds + Maris Ambats, 1975; no patents filed; Reynolds called it "a real biofeedback tool." $15M sales by Dec 1975.
- [LLNL "Look Back": the mood ring's origin](https://st.llnl.gov/news/look-back/llnl-and-mood-ring): the underlying tech came from Robert Parker, a weapons-program engineer at Lawrence Livermore (1958-), who studied liquid crystal films for temperature diagnostics and encapsulated them in gelatin (National Cash Register method) to prevent oxidation. His products: novelty cups, water-temperature indicators, digital thermometers. 1975: Josh Reynolds walked into Parker's shop proposing the jewelry application "to help people control their emotions" via temperature feedback, after Wall Street stress led him to biofeedback.
- [ThoughtCo](https://www.thoughtco.com/how-do-mood-rings-work-604307): cholesteric liquid crystals twist with temperature; twisting changes molecular structure → alters reflected/absorbed wavelengths → color. Scale: black = coolest, violet = warmest. Modern stones: liquid crystal strip under acrylic/glass.
- [HowStuffWorks](https://www.howstuffworks.com/mood-ring-colors.htm): neutral blue-green at ~98.6°F nominal; vendor color charts map hues to claimed emotions.

### Physiology: what finger temperature tracks
- [Mayo Clinic Proceedings review](https://www.mayoclinicproceedings.org/article/S0025-6196(11)61930-7/fulltext): sympathetic neural control of skin blood flow = noradrenergic vasoconstrictor system + active vasodilator system; palms/fingers innervated only by vasoconstrictor nerves.
- [Advances in Physiology Education, sympathetic release test](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/advan.00095.2013): finger skin temperature is a clinical measure of sympathetic vasoconstrictor tone; used to diagnose disorders (Raynaud's = hyperreactive peripheral vasoconstriction).
- [Neural control of human skin blood flow (Autonomic Neuroscience)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0165183890901286): "mental stress, arousal and deep breaths" cause cutaneous reflex vasoconstriction/vasodilatation via sympathetic traffic.
- [PMC4593810](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4593810/): thermoregulatory reflex chain: cold-sensitive skin thermosensors → hypothalamic-preoptic integration → sympathetic vasoconstriction.

### Validity: what the temperature signal can and cannot do
- [Spectral characteristics of skin temperature indicate peripheral stress-response, Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback, DOI 10.1007/BF01543790](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01543790): high-res skin temp shows low-amplitude oscillations (~0.04°C); stress changes the oscillatory frequency/amplitude far more than mean temperature (mean changes limited to ~2.4%). The RHYTHM is the sensitive stress indicator, not the mean level.
- [JMIR mHealth validation study](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2021/2/e19210): continuous body-surface thermometry is feasible (ICC 0.96-0.98 calibrated), but a systematic review found 94% of contact-thermometry studies lack calibration detail → most skin-temp data is under-validated. Ambient temperature confounds; wrist skin-temp rhythm tracks circadian phase.
