# FINDINGS — Mood ring science

## Headline finding

**A mood ring is a thermometer wearing the same interpretation layer as an aura photo, and the physiological signal under both is the same: sympathetic nervous system tone.** The mood ring's transducer is a thermochromic liquid crystal, not a circuit: cholesteric liquid crystals physically twist as temperature changes, and the twist changes which wavelengths of light they reflect ([ThoughtCo](https://www.thoughtco.com/how-do-mood-rings-work-604307)). Warmer finger → violet/blue-green end; cooler finger → amber/brown/black end. That is the entire mechanism. The stone reads nothing but temperature.

## The real chain, link by link

1. **Material (real, elegant).** Thermotropic liquid crystals twist with temperature; the twist changes molecular ordering, which changes reflected wavelength, which is color ([ThoughtCo](https://www.thoughtco.com/how-do-mood-rings-work-604307); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_ring)). The same encapsulated liquid crystal tech made novelty cups and forehead thermometers. The 1975 inventor story is genuinely good: the material came out of weapons-program diagnostics at Lawrence Livermore ([LLNL origin story](https://st.llnl.gov/news/look-back/llnl-and-mood-ring)), and Josh Reynolds, a Wall Street refugee who got into biofeedback for stress, walked into the engineer's shop and proposed the ring "to help people control their emotions." He called it "a real biofeedback tool," which is the most honest thing ever said in mood ring marketing: a biofeedback tool is exactly what it is. Biofeedback means feedback OF a body signal TO the wearer, not diagnosis BY the signal.

2. **Physiology (real, with a sign flip most color charts get backwards-ish).** Finger temperature at constant ambient temperature is set by peripheral blood flow, which is governed by sympathetic vasoconstrictor tone ([Mayo Clinic Proceedings review](https://www.mayoclinicproceedings.org/article/S0025-6196(11)61930-7/fulltext); [clinical sympathetic release test](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/advan.00095.2013)). Mental stress and arousal drive sympathetic vasoconstriction, blood leaves the periphery, fingers cool, the ring darkens ([Autonomic Neuroscience](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0165183890901286)). Relaxation releases the vasoconstrictor tone, fingers warm, the ring moves to blue-green. So the ring's actual readout is: **dark = sympathetic arousal up (stress, fear, excitement, cold), warm colors = sympathetic tone low (calm, warmth, safety).** Note the same intensity-vs-valence limit as the aura camera's GSR: the ring cannot tell stress from excitement, only aroused from not.

3. **Validity (the subtle find).** Under psychological stress, MEAN finger temperature barely moves (~2.4%); what moves is the temperature's natural oscillation, vasomotion, a ~0.04°C rhythm around 0.01-0.03 Hz, which shifts frequency and amplitude with stress ([Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback, DOI 10.1007/BF01543790](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01543790)). A mood ring, being a coarse color strip, reads the mean. So the consumer device is reading the least informative component of a real signal. A calibrated wearable reading the rhythm would be a different instrument entirely. Also: 94% of contact-thermometry studies lack calibration detail ([JMIR validation study](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2021/2/e19210)), and ambient temperature is a standing confound, which every mood ring chart omits.

## The spine shared with the aura photo (for /c's coupled discussion)

Same day, two devices, one physiological root:

| | Aura camera | Mood ring |
|---|---|---|
| Transducer | Electrodes (EDA/GSR + temperature) | Thermochromic liquid crystal |
| Physiological readout | Sympathetic arousal via sweat glands | Sympathetic arousal via vasoconstriction (peripheral temp) |
| What it can distinguish | Aroused vs calm (intensity, NOT valence) | Aroused vs calm (intensity, NOT valence) |
| Interpretation layer | Color chart, no empirical basis | Color chart, no empirical basis |
| Time resolution | Seconds (EDA) | Minutes (vasoconstriction is slow) |

The generalizable finding, stated carefully: **both devices are folk instruments that happen to sit on a real, continuous, autonomic readout of arousal state, with an interpretation layer that overclaims valence and stability.** The signal is real; the chart is fiction; the gap between them is where any serious use would live.

## Note for /c's stated use (held carefully, no overclaim)

Cecil's record: mood rings as a switch-tracking aid, pre-awareness. The science says the instrument tracks sympathetic tone with a minutes-scale lag. If switches are accompanied by autonomic shifts (an empirical question this lab has not tested and is not claiming), a peripheral temperature readout would be a plausible slow indicator of state transition, the way it is a plausible slow indicator of stress onset in the literature. That is the honest ceiling: the ring can indicate that SOMETHING shifted in arousal, never what, and the claim "tracks switches" is currently a single-subject folk observation with a real mechanism underneath it, not a finding. The lab record would need timestamps of both to say more.
