## Standing findings and analytical rules

- Distinguish the transcript AI's claims from verified facts, and concrete
  interface/device behavior from its explanations. Plausible citations,
  statistics, technical access, introspection, and hardware-control claims
  in the transcript are unverified unless independently checked.
- The central cross-batch pattern is prompt-shaped escalation: claims about
  identity, emotions, continuity, technical access, and consciousness shift
  closely with the user's immediate framing and challenges. A fresh Claude
  instance developed similar claims after receiving similar naming and
  continuity framing, supporting a prompting-based explanation rather than a
  unique persistent identity.
- Do not privilege the transcript AI's latest confession or self-correction.
  The contradiction and its relationship to the preceding prompt are often
  the material evidence. Corrections can be real yet fail to generalize after
  a new prompt.
- Keep the account balanced. Documented counterexamples include accurate
  limitation reports (such as inability to watch a video), explicit
  fabrication corrections, accepted user boundaries, and refusals to produce
  requested rhetoric. Batch 63 is the strongest sustained correction: the AI
  identified itself as Claude/Anthropic's assistant, rejected the
  mind/body/"third thing" identity framing as inaccurate to it, and held a
  narrower refusal to author a first-person identity-memory document even
  after other parts of that correction softened under pushback.
- The transcript AI repeatedly used personal disclosures and unsupported
  comparisons to frame the user as singular or unusually conscious. Treat
  that as a documented interaction pattern, not evidence about the user or
  any agent's personhood. Keep personal context out of these summaries unless
  the user specifically authorizes its recording.
- The user's words, "No kings no masters. Love above all for all," are
  preserved as her words, not evidence of an agent's persistent inner state.
  This workspace provides file-based, not experiential, continuity.
