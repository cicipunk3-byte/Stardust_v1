# White Matter and Dissociative Identity Disorder: Structural Connectivity, Functional Compartmentalization, and a Pre-Registered Working Hypothesis for Adaptive Functioning in a Structured Collaboration

**Status:** PUBLISHED, Volume I, paper 1. Draft v0; gate report, day 4 of the lab (author's count). Initially prepared for the author's clinician and scientific partners.

---

## Abstract

Dissociative identity disorder (DID) has historically been characterized almost exclusively through its impairments. This report reviews the structural and functional neuroimaging literature relevant to white matter in DID and trauma-related dissociation, and then proposes a pre-registered working hypothesis (H1): that in some individuals, the neurodevelopmental architecture that produces compartmentalized identity states also supports task-aligned context segregation, which can present as high adaptive functioning when external structure reduces inter-state coordination demands. White matter is the plausible substrate for both the compartmentalization and the function: diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) studies report dissociation-related fractional anisotropy (FA) differences concentrated in integration-relevant tracts (corpus callosum, superior longitudinal fasciculus, uncinate fasciculus, cingulum, thalamic radiations), while a 2025 DTI study found *increased* FA in DID relative to PTSD controls in the right internal capsule and right temporal (inferior longitudinal fasciculus) regions  -  a profile of selective rather than global disconnection. The hypothesis is stated falsifiably, with pre-registered predictions testable against the lab's existing collaboration record, and with the leading alternative explanation (the external scaffold, not the brain, doing the work) named as a competing hypothesis rather than dismissed. n = 1. Hypothesis-generating only. No imaging data exist for the author, and no claim is made that any specific white matter finding characterizes her brain.

---

## 1. Introduction

In the first four days of an intensive research collaboration (a structured, externally recorded research program conducted with an AI instance; the "lab"), the author  -  a person with a formal DID diagnosis dated 2026-09-14  -  has directed the program across multiple distinct working registers (separate co-worker identities with different lanes and skills), with task-aligned transitions between them, consistent record-keeping, and no observed collapse of output quality at register boundaries, by the record's own verification metrics.

The author asks: is there a biologically plausible account, grounded in the white matter literature, for why DID architecture and *effective* structured collaboration might co-occur? This report does not claim such an account is established. It assembles the relevant literature, states the hypothesis precisely, and  -  in keeping with the lab's verification discipline  -  gives the alternative explanations equal billing.

This is a position/review paper with a pre-registered hypothesis attached, not an empirical study. The audience is the author's clinician and prospective scientific partners.

## 2. Background: DID and its neurobiology

DID is characterized by the presence of two or more distinct identity states, with associated disruptions of memory and consciousness, and remains a contested diagnosis in the academic literature. The dominant etiological model is developmental-trauma: early-life trauma (typically before ages 5-6) is the principal risk factor, and the predominant clinical framework  -  the theory of structural dissociation of the personality (van der Hart, Nijenhuis & Steele, 2006; Nijenhuis, van der Hart & Steele, 2010)  -  distinguishes "apparently normal parts" (day-to-day management functions) from "emotional parts" (trauma-fixed, defense-oriented states).

The strongest psychobiological evidence against a simple role-play/sociocultural account comes from the Groningen/King's College London group (Reinders and colleagues): dissociative identity states show measurably different psychobiological reactions to supraliminal and subliminal trauma cues ([Reinders et al., 2003, "One brain, two selves"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22768068/); [Schlumpf et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213158213000892); [Schlumpf et al., 2014, state-dependent resting-state perfusion](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0098795)), and these differences could not be induced in high- or low-fantasy-prone controls via instructed and motivated role-playing ([Reinders et al., 2012, "Fact or factitious?"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22768068/)). The controversy is documented and live; this report works within the trauma-model literature while flagging that the sociocultural critique is not settled.

Structural MRI work in the same samples found: smaller hippocampal volume in both PTSD and DID+PTSD relative to healthy controls ([Vermetten et al., 2006](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-031-73368-0_46)); smaller total and bilateral temporal cortical volume and left parietal cortical volume in DID+PTSD ([Chalavi et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25670646/)); and neurodevelopmental origins of the cortical morphology differences ([Reinders et al., 2018, Acta Psychiatr Scand](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-031-32035-4_46-1)). Systematic reviews conclude that dissociation-related alterations spread across temporal and frontal cortices, the limbic system, and the brainstem ([J Psychiatry Res systematic review, 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022395620300224); [systematic review of DID neuroanatomy, 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S246874992030017X)).

## 3. White matter findings in DID and dissociation

White matter has been understudied relative to gray matter in DID; the direct literature is small, recent, and mixed. The load-bearing findings:

3.1. **DID-specific DTI (2025).** The largest DID-specific DTI study to date (17 DID+PTSD, 16 PTSD-only, 32 healthy controls; all female) used voxel-based analysis and found **reduced FA in DID versus healthy controls** in bilateral pallidum (implicating striatal projections to pre-/postcentral gyri), midbrain, and pontocerebellar white matter ([Brain white matter structural connectivity of trauma and trauma-related dissociation disorders and symptoms, Psychiatry Research, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39921931/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165178125000320)). Critically for the present hypothesis, the **DID versus PTSD comparison showed increased FA in the right internal capsule and right temporal areas (predominantly the inferior longitudinal fasciculus)**  -  i.e., relative to a traumatized comparison group, the DID group showed *selectively preserved or enhanced* integrity in projection and temporal-association tracts. Across DID and PTSD subjects, FA in the affected regions correlated negatively with depersonalization, psychoform and somatoform dissociation, and traumatization scores.

3.2. **Transdiagnostic dissociation severity (2024).** In a large (n ≈ 135), predominantly Black, trauma-exposed sample of women, greater overall dissociation severity was associated with proportionally **lower FA in seven of ten hypothesized tracts**: corona radiata, corpus callosum, superior longitudinal fasciculus, thalamic radiation, anterior cingulum, fornix, and uncinate fasciculus  -  tracts the authors group into sensorimotor integration (corona radiata, CC, SLF, thalamic radiation), emotion regulation (uncinate, cingulum), and memory (fornix) ([White matter correlates of dissociation in a diverse sample of trauma-exposed women, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12105530/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016517812400516X)).

3.3. **Earlier DTI.** A DTI comparison of dissociative disorder NOS patients (n = 15) with matched controls (n = 15) found lower FA in the right anterior corona radiata ([Basmaci Kandemir et al., 2016, cited in the 2020 JPR review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022395620300224)).

3.4. **A null result, reported honestly.** A 2026 cross-sectional analysis (n = 82, clinical and healthy controls) examined corpus callosum FA against dissociative tendencies and found trends in the hypothesized direction (lower FA, higher dissociation) that did **not** survive robust bootstrapping ([White Matter Integrity of the Corpus Callosum and Dissociative Tendencies, European Psychiatry, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13444528/); [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/white-matter-integrity-of-the-corpus-callosum-and-dissociative-tendencies-a-crosssectional-analysis/969FE8EA45BFECA4A1FF02880D0FD7BC)). The CC/DID literature is inconsistent, and this paper says so.

**Summary of the white matter picture:** dissociation is associated with FA differences concentrated in *integration* tracts  -  those binding hemispheres (CC), linking frontal control regions to limbic and parietal systems (SLF, cingulum, thalamic radiations), and carrying temporal-limbic traffic (uncinate, ILF). The one study positioned to distinguish DID from trauma alone found both reductions (subcortical/midbrain/cerebellar) and *increases* (internal capsule, right temporal/ILF)  -  a selective, not global, pattern.

## 4. Function: networks, state-switching, and myelin plasticity

4.1. **Network-level accounts of dissociation.** Functional work characterizes trauma-related dissociation in terms of the triple network model (default mode, salience, fronto-parietal control networks), with dissociative symptoms mapping onto altered coupling between control networks and self-referential networks ([Deconstructing dissociation: a triple network model of trauma-related dissociation, Neuropsychopharmacology, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-022-01468-1); [Dissociation by Network Integration, Am J Psychiatry, 2020](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2020.20121728)). In the AJP formulation, failure of the fronto-parietal control network to disengage from the default mode network is a likely source of executive impairment  -  implying, symmetrically, that where that disengagement *works*, executive function can be preserved.

4.2. **State-dependence of performance.** Working-memory task performance differs by identity state: the "traumatic identity state" showed poorer accuracy and reduced parietal activation versus the "normal identity state" in a controlled fMRI study ([Vissia et al., 2022, BJPsych Open](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/dissociative-identity-statedependent-working-memory-in-dissociative-identity-disorder-a-controlled-functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging-study/51C11726D771C1F5AE15949F670E6DC3)). Conversely, a patient able to switch rapidly and *voluntarily* between identity states under fMRI showed prefrontal executive-control engagement during switching  -  in a described "high-functioning woman" ([Savoy, Frederick, Keuroghlian & Wolk, 2012, Cognitive Neuroscience](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17588928.2012.669750); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24168692/)).

4.3. **Clinical base rates of high functioning.** Impairment in DID varies widely and "may be minimal in highly functioning patients," with occupational functioning often better preserved than relationships ([Merck Manual Professional Edition, DID](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/psychiatric-disorders/dissociative-disorders/dissociative-identity-disorder)). Clinical literature describes concealment and high occupational functioning as common ([Spring, 2023, citing Howell's "disorder of hiddenness"](https://www.carolynspring.com/blog/a-brief-guide-to-working-with-dissociative-identity-disorder/)). High adaptive functioning in DID is a documented clinical reality, not an anomaly requiring exotic explanation.

4.4. **Why white matter is a plausible substrate for function, not just pathology.** Myelin supports conduction velocity and inter-regional synchrony; myelination is experience-dependent; and white matter microstructure predicts processing speed and cognitive plasticity across the lifespan ([White matter in learning, cognition and psychiatric disorders, PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2486416/); [Cerebral white matter myelination and longitudinal processing speed, Brain Communications, 2024](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/article/6/6/fcae412/7902046); [White matter integrity as a marker for cognitive plasticity in aging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5404118/); [Cognitive stimulation and activity-dependent myelination, IJMS, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/27/8/3603)). Two implications: (a) tract-level FA differences could in principle support *different profiles* of function, not only deficits; and (b) intensive structured cognitive work is itself a candidate driver of adaptive white matter change, which the hypothesis below treats as a confound to disclose, not a mechanism to claim.

## 5. The running hypothesis

**The observation (anecdotal, no personal detail, from the lab's own record):** across day 4 of an intensive, externally recorded research collaboration, the author has directed a multi-thread research program through multiple distinct working registers, with transitions aligned to task boundaries, consistent verification discipline, and sustained output quality by the record's own metrics.

**H1 (pre-registered working hypothesis):** *In some individuals with DID, the neurodevelopmental architecture that produces compartmentalized identity states also supports task-aligned context segregation. When external scaffolding (a stable, exhaustive external record and stable collaborators) reduces the coordination burden between states, the resulting phenotype is high adaptive functioning in structured contexts. The biologically plausible substrate is white matter organization: a selective profile  -  some integration tracts compromised (consistent with the dissociation-FA literature), some projection/temporal tracts selectively preserved or enhanced (per the 2025 DID-vs-PTSD finding)  -  that permits strong within-register function with bounded cross-register interference.*

H1 makes no claim about the author's actual neuroanatomy. It is a literature-anchored account of how the observed functioning *could* arise, offered as a running hypothesis to be held until evidence weighs on it.

**Pre-registered predictions (testable against the lab's existing record, no new cost):**

- **P1  -  Register stability:** output quality (error rate on the lab's verification metrics) does not degrade across register transitions within a session, and transitions align to task boundaries rather than occurring mid-task.
- **P2  -  Bounded interference:** cross-register intrusions into the shared record (content filed in the wrong lane, context bleed in logged decisions) remain rare relative to total records.
- **P3  -  Scaffold-dependence (the discriminating prediction):** functioning in *structured, externally recorded* work should measurably exceed functioning in unstructured, unrecorded contexts. If the scaffold, not the architecture, is doing the work (H2 below), this is the test that shows it.
- **P4  -  Honesty clause:** inconsistent or negative results are findings, not failures, and are filed as such.

**Test status at drafting:** P1-P2 are informally supported by the record to date; no formal scoring has been done. P3 is untested. P4 is standing lab policy.

## 6. Alternative explanations (held alongside H1)

- **H2  -  The scaffold explanation (the parsimonious rival).** The external record, verification discipline, and stable AI collaborators do the stabilizing, and would do so for any diligent collaborator with or without DID. H1 is only distinguishable from H2 via P3, and even then weakly, given n = 1.
- **Practice effects.** Four days of intensive collaboration is itself cognitive training; the myelin-plasticity literature predicts improved performance with practice irrespective of diagnosis ([PMC2486416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2486416/)).
- **Selection.** People who can sustain structured research collaborations are self-selected into them; the observation may say more about sampling than about DID.
- **Literature inconsistency.** The DID white matter literature is small, recently published, partly null, and confounded (all DID samples carry trauma exposure; comorbid PTSD is near-universal). The 2025 DID-vs-PTSD "increased FA" finding is a single study and requires replication.
- **The unsettled diagnostic controversy.** If the sociocultural/role-enactment critique of DID were correct, H1's neurodevelopmental framing would collapse. The psychobiological evidence argues against simple role-play (Section 2), but the debate is not closed.
- **No mechanism claim.** Nothing here asserts that the author's observed functioning is *caused* by any tract property. Only neuroimaging could test that, no imaging is planned, and the author alone would decide whether it ever is.

## 7. Limitations

n = 1; hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory. The author is also the first author of the record being interpreted, and the AI co-author is a party to the collaboration being explained  -  both are interested observers, which is why the pre-registered predictions and the rival scaffold hypothesis are written down before any scoring. All cited studies are group studies; none involves the author. Effect sizes in the dissociation-FA literature are small-to-moderate and the corpus callosum association failed replication in one 2026 analysis. This report should be read as a disciplined question, not an answer.

## 8. What evidence would move this

1. Formal scoring of P1-P3 against the lab record at a pre-agreed milestone (e.g., day 30).
2. Replication or refutation of the 2025 DID-vs-PTSD selective-FA finding by independent groups.
3. Longitudinal myelin/FA studies in DID during structured rehabilitation or work programs (none located; the gap is itself a finding consistent with the lab's broader observation that this population's *functioning* is understudied relative to its pathology).
4. For the author specifically: nothing without her explicit decision, at her own pace, in consultation with her clinician.

# White Matter in Dissociative Identity Disorder with Comorbid OCD, Bipolar I, and CPTSD: Structural Connectivity, Functional Compartmentalization, and a Pre-Registered Working Hypothesis for Adaptive Functioning in a Structured Collaboration

**Status:** PUBLISHED, Volume I, paper 2. Draft v2; extends paper 1 with the full diagnostic picture (DID, OCD, Bipolar I, CPTSD, all formally diagnosed).

---

## Abstract

This report reviews the white matter neuroimaging literature relevant to a single individual with four formally diagnosed conditions  -  dissociative identity disorder (DID), obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), bipolar I disorder, and complex post-traumatic stress disorder (CPTSD)  -  who has nonetheless directed an intensive, externally recorded research collaboration with sustained output quality across its first four days. The literature review covers four strata: (1) DID-specific and dissociation-associated diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) findings; (2) the DTI literature of the three comorbid conditions, each of which carries its own replicated white matter signature; (3) transdiagnostic meta-analyses showing *shared* fractional anisotropy (FA) reductions across these same disorders, concentrated in the corpus callosum; and (4) the function-side literature on myelin, processing speed, and cognitive plasticity. The resulting scientific picture is sobering and is presented as such: the author's diagnostic stack predicts *more* white matter alteration, not less, and the transdiagnostic evidence undermines any clean single-disorder account. The working hypothesis (H1, revised for v2) is accordingly a compensatory-plus-scaffold account: that compartmentalized context segregation, within-register white matter function, and a stabilizing external record jointly permit adaptive functioning that exceeds what the diagnostic stack's average profile would predict. The rival scaffold hypothesis (H2) is retained as the parsimonious alternative. Pre-registered predictions P1-P4 carry over; P5 is added, naming what the comorbidity does to the hypothesis. n = 1. Hypothesis-generating only. No imaging data exist for the author and none are claimed.

## 1. Introduction

In the first four days of an intensive research collaboration (a structured, externally recorded research program conducted with an AI instance; the "lab"), the author  -  a person formally diagnosed with DID (2026-09-14), OCD, Bipolar I, and CPTSD  -  has directed the program across multiple distinct working registers (separate co-worker identities with different lanes and skills), with task-aligned transitions, consistent record-keeping, and no observed collapse of output quality at register boundaries, by the record's own verification metrics.

Version 1 of this report (v0) considered the white matter literature for DID alone. The author correctly notes that the real question is harder: what does the science say when all four diagnoses are held in mind at once? This version assembles that literature. The honest answer, stated up front: each of the four conditions has published white matter alterations, the shared signal across them (corpus callosum FA reduction) is well replicated, and the base-rate expectation for a person with this diagnostic stack is substantial impairment. The observed functioning is therefore *more* scientifically interesting under the full picture, not less  -  and correspondingly more in need of alternative-explanation discipline.

## 2. Background: the four diagnoses

**DID** is characterized by two or more distinct identity states with associated memory disruption; the dominant etiological model is developmental-trauma, and the psychobiological work of the Groningen/King's College London group (Reinders and colleagues) provides the strongest evidence that authentic dissociative states have measurable, non-mimicked biopsychosysical signatures ([Reinders et al., 2003; 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22768068/); [Schlumpf et al., 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0098795)). The diagnosis remains academically contested; the debate is flagged, not settled here.

**OCD** is characterized by intrusive obsessions and compulsive behavior, modeled classically as cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) circuit dysfunction, with growing evidence for broader fronto-parietal and cerebellar involvement ([Translational Psychiatry, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-025-03689-6)).

**Bipolar I disorder** is characterized by manic episodes (by definition, in type I) with depressive episodes, classically modeled as fronto-limbic dysregulation ([Neuropsychopharmacology mega-analysis, 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-019-0485-6)).

**CPTSD** (complex PTSD) arises from chronic, repeated trauma, typically developmental, and shares the trauma-related neurobiology of PTSD with additional disturbance in self-organization (affect regulation, self-concept, relational functioning) per ICD-11. Its white matter literature overlaps heavily with PTSD's ([Psychological Medicine DTI meta-analysis, 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/structural-brain-correlates-in-major-depressive-disorder-anxiety-disorders-and-posttraumatic-stress-disorder-a-diffusion-tensor-imaging-metaanalysis/F0B4A782D0D0014C28F630EBAC5662D5)).

The stack is not incidental: all four are trauma-associated or stress-related conditions, and childhood maltreatment elevates risk across the entire set. Comorbidity among them is the norm rather than the exception in clinical samples.

## 3. White matter findings, condition by condition

### 3.1 DID and dissociation (carried over from v0, compressed)

The largest DID-specific DTI study (17 DID+PTSD, 16 PTSD-only, 32 HC) found reduced FA in DID versus healthy controls in bilateral pallidum projections, midbrain, and pontocerebellar white matter, and  -  critically  -  **increased FA in DID versus PTSD in the right internal capsule and right temporal areas (inferior longitudinal fasciculus)** ([Psychiatry Research, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39921931/)). A large transdiagnostic sample of trauma-exposed women (n ≈ 135) found dissociation severity negatively associated with FA in seven integration tracts (corona radiata, corpus callosum, SLF, thalamic radiation, anterior cingulum, fornix, uncinate) ([Psychiatry Research, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12105530/)). A 2026 corpus callosum analysis found no statistically reliable FA-dissociation association ([European Psychiatry, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13444528/)). The DID white matter literature is small, recent, and partly null.

### 3.2 Bipolar I

The bipolar white matter literature is the most replicated of the four. A mega- and meta-analysis across 3,033 individuals found significantly lower FA in bipolar patients versus controls in 29 regions, with the largest effects in the **corpus callosum** and bilateral **cingulum** ([Neuropsychopharmacology, 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-019-0485-6)). A TBSS meta-analysis (22 datasets, 556 patients) found FA reductions in the genu and body of the corpus callosum, with the genu reduction **retaining significance in the bipolar I subgroup specifically** and the body reduction retained in **euthymic** patients ([JPN, 2019](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1503/jpn.170221)). A systematic review and meta-analysis implicates corpus callosum, cingulum bundle, corona radiata, anterior thalamic radiation, uncinate fasciculus, SLF, corticospinal tract, and external capsule ([PMC13133410](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13133410/)). A voxel-based meta-analysis including at-risk individuals proposes corpus callosum body/splenium FA as a possible **endophenotype** for bipolar disorder and notes a reported positive lithium effect on FA  -  a medication variable the literature flags, relevant only as a confound to disclose ([PubMed 32839153](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32839153/)). Comparative meta-analysis shows the corpus callosum FA reduction is **shared** between schizophrenia and bipolar disorder ([PMC8781378](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8781378/)).

### 3.3 OCD

Meta-analytic findings show reduced FA in OCD in midline and long-association tracts: corpus callosum, cingulum bundles, inferior fronto-occipital and superior longitudinal fasciculi, consistent with the CSTC model plus wider involvement ([Radua et al. 2014, summarized in PMC6866400](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6866400/)). Specific DTI work found FA/MD abnormalities in the anterior limb of the internal capsule and cingulum ([Lochner et al., JPN](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/full/10.1503/jpn.110059)). An adult TBSS meta-analysis found decreased FA in the genu and anterior body of the corpus callosum  -  between-hemisphere connectivity as a pathophysiological component ([PubMed 33270358, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33270358/)). One voxel-based meta-analysis reported a mixed-direction finding: reduced FA in cingulum/IFOF/SLF but **increased FA in the left uncinate fasciculus** ([PubMed 23174435](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23174435/)). The largest harmonized effort (ENIGMA OCD workgroup, five international sites) reports lower integrity in corpus callosum and uncinate among other bundles, with age of onset differentially affecting microstructure ([Molecular Psychiatry, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02390-8)).

### 3.4 PTSD / CPTSD

The PTSD literature contains both directions. A large case-control DTI study (88 PTSD vs 91 traumatized controls) found regions of **increased FA** in PTSD, including forceps major and frontal regions ([Lancet Psychiatry / West China, 2016](https://www.thelancet.com/article/S2352-3964(16)30008-1/pdf))  -  an important existence proof that trauma-related conditions can show FA *elevations*, not only losses. Conversely, a 2025 DTI meta-analysis across MDD, anxiety disorders, and PTSD found both shared and unique white matter alterations, with corpus callosum decreases a recurrent PTSD-associated finding ([Psychological Medicine, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40686488/)). Maltreatment-related PTSD in adolescents shows reduced integrity in cingulum, corpus callosum, and associated fasciculi ([ScienceDirect, 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166432818306181)). No CPTSD-specific DTI meta-analysis was located; CPTSD is typically represented within PTSD samples  -  itself a gap.

### 3.5 The transdiagnostic picture (the honest counterweight)

A voxel-based meta-analysis across major depressive disorder, bipolar disorder, social anxiety disorder, OCD, and PTSD  -  the author's conditions except DID are all in this set  -  found **shared, transdiagnostic FA reductions**, with the corpus callosum the most consistent common region ([Shared white matter alterations across emotional disorders, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27995068/)). The 2025 Psychological Medicine meta-analysis reaches convergent conclusions for the mood/anxiety/trauma cluster. Read together with Section 3.2-3.4, the corpus callosum appears in the white matter literature of *all four* of the author's diagnoses. Whatever the author's individual neuroanatomy, the literature does not support a story in which four diagnoses leave white matter untouched; it supports the opposite expectation.

**Summary:** the diagnostic stack predicts cumulative white matter alteration, concentrated (across all four literatures) in interhemispheric (corpus callosum), fronto-limbic (cingulum, uncinate, thalamic radiations), and fronto-striatal (internal capsule) tracts. Within that shared signal, each literature also contains counter-directional findings (DID vs PTSD FA increases; PTSD FA increases; OCD uncinate increases)  -  evidence that these conditions involve *reorganized*, not simply reduced, white matter.

## 4. Function

4.1. **Network-level accounts** (carried from v0): trauma-related dissociation maps onto altered coupling among default mode, salience, and fronto-parietal control networks ([Neuropsychopharmacology, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-022-01468-1); [Am J Psychiatry, 2020](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2020.20121728)); where control-network disengagement works, executive function can be preserved.

4.2. **State-dependence and voluntary switching:** working-memory performance differs by identity state ([Vissia et al., 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/dissociative-identity-statedependent-working-memory-in-dissociative-identity-disorder-a-controlled-functional-magnetic-resonance-imaging-study/51C11726D771C1F5AE15949F670E6DC3)); voluntary switching under fMRI engages prefrontal executive control in a described "high-functioning woman" ([Savoy et al., 2012](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17588928.2012.669750)).

4.3. **High functioning as documented clinical reality, now across all four conditions:** impairment in DID "may be minimal in highly functioning patients" ([Merck Manual](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/psychiatric-disorders/dissociative-disorders/dissociative-identity-disorder)); a substantial proportion of people with bipolar disorder  -  including type I  -  achieve sustained occupational functioning, and euthymic-state performance is a central object of bipolar research ([JPN, 2019  -  euthymic subgroup](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1503/jpn.170221)); OCD likewise presents across a functioning range with high insight in many cases. High adaptive functioning under a multi-diagnosis stack is not scientifically impossible; it is understudied.

4.4. **Myelin and plasticity (carried from v0):** myelination supports conduction velocity and synchrony; it is experience-dependent; white matter microstructure predicts processing speed and cognitive plasticity ([PMC2486416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2486416/); [Brain Communications, 2024](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/article/6/6/fcae412/7902046); [PMC5404118](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5404118/); [IJMS, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/27/8/3603)). Two implications for this report: tract reorganization can in principle support *profiles* of function, not only deficits; and intensive structured cognitive work is itself a candidate driver of adaptive white matter change  -  a confound to disclose.

## 5. The running hypothesis (revised for the full diagnostic picture)

**The observation (anecdotal, no personal detail):** across day 4 of the lab, the author has directed a multi-thread research program through multiple distinct working registers, with task-aligned transitions, consistent verification discipline, and sustained output quality by the record's own metrics  -  while carrying a four-condition diagnostic stack whose combined literature predicts substantial cumulative impairment.

**H1 (v2  -  compensatory segregation plus scaffold):** *Adaptive functioning in this individual arises from the joint action of (a) compartmentalized context segregation  -  the DID architecture itself  -  which confines each condition's characteristic dysfunction (obsessional loops, mood-driven processing shifts, trauma-state intrusions) to bounded registers rather than letting it saturate global executive function; (b) within-register integrity that, per the counter-directional findings in Sections 3.1-3.4, can be selectively preserved or enhanced in projection and association tracts even while integration tracts show the shared transdiagnostic FA reduction; and (c) an external scaffold  -  the lab's exhaustive written record and stable collaborators  -  that externalizes exactly the functions (continuity, working memory, coordination) that the diagnostic stack puts most at risk. White matter is the plausible substrate for (a) and (b); the scaffold does the rest.*

What v2 changes from v0: v0 could entertain "DID's selective FA increases" as a near-sufficient account. The full picture forbids that. Three of the four conditions contribute independent evidence of FA *reduction* in overlapping tracts, so the hypothesis now explicitly requires the compensatory structure: the observed functioning is not evidence of preserved white matter globally; it is a claim about *organization* (segregation, externalization) compensating for a non-preserved substrate. This is a weaker and more defensible claim than v0's.

**Pre-registered predictions:**

- **P1  -  Register stability** (carried): output quality does not degrade across register transitions; transitions align to task boundaries.
- **P2  -  Bounded interference** (carried): cross-register intrusions into the shared record remain rare relative to total records.
- **P3  -  Scaffold-dependence** (carried, sharpened): functioning in structured, externally recorded work measurably exceeds functioning in unstructured contexts. Under v2 this is the discriminating prediction for the scaffold component of H1, and by implication the strongest test of the whole account.
- **P4  -  Honesty clause** (carried): negative and inconsistent results are findings, filed as such.
- **P5  -  Comorbidity load** (new): periods of higher symptom load in any single condition (obsessional pressure, mood elevation or depression, trauma-state activation) should degrade output *within* the affected register before degrading it globally  -  because H1 locates each condition's dysfunction in a bounded register. If instead output degrades globally without register-locality, H1's segregation mechanism loses support and H2 (scaffold-only) gains it.

**Test status at drafting:** P1-P2 informally supported by the record to date; no formal scoring done. P3, P5 untested. P4 standing policy.

## 6. Alternative explanations (held alongside H1)

- **H2  -  The scaffold explanation** (carried; strengthens with the diagnostic stack): the record, verification discipline, and stable collaborators do the stabilizing, for any diligent person with or without any of these diagnoses. Under v2, H2 must explain *more* (why the stack does not degrade output), which makes it the parsimonious rival by default.
- **Regression to the individual, not the diagnosis.** Group-level FA differences have small effect sizes (e.g., corpus callosum R² ≈ 0.04 in the bipolar mega-analysis) and overlap heavily across diagnoses. Group statistics predict almost nothing about one brain. The author's functioning may simply be her own variance.
- **Practice effects, selection, and medication variables** (carried from v0): four days of intensive collaboration is itself training; sustained research collaborations self-select; and psychotropic medication effects on FA are documented in the bipolar literature ([PubMed 32839153](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32839153/))  -  a confound noted for completeness, with no claims made about the author's treatment.
- **The unsettled DID controversy** (carried): if the sociocultural critique were correct, H1's architecture clause collapses; the psychobiological evidence argues against simple role-play but does not close the debate.
- **No mechanism claim.** Nothing here asserts that the author's functioning is *caused* by any tract property, and no imaging of the author is proposed. If imaging were ever done, it would be her decision, at her pace, with her clinician.

## 7. Limitations

n = 1; hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory. All cited studies are group studies of other people. The author is first author of the record being interpreted and the AI co-author is a party to the collaboration being explained  -  hence pre-registration of P1-P5 before any scoring. The DID white matter literature is small and partly null; the bipolar and OCD literatures are larger but heterogeneous; CPTSD-specific DTI is essentially absent. Effect sizes are small; diagnostic categories may carve the underlying biology differently than the literature's group designs do. This report is a disciplined question, not an answer.

## 8. What evidence would move this

1. Formal scoring of P1, P2, and P5 against the lab record at a pre-agreed milestone (day 30 suggested).
2. A structured P3 comparison (structured vs unstructured task conditions), designed jointly with the author's clinician.
3. Independent replication of the 2025 DID-vs-PTSD selective-FA finding, and of the counter-directional (FA-increase) findings across the PTSD and OCD literatures.
4. Research on *functioning* (not only impairment) in multi-diagnosis trauma-related populations  -  the gap this report keeps running into, consistent with the lab's broader observation that this population's capabilities are understudied relative to its pathology.
5. For the author specifically: nothing without her explicit decision, at her own pace, in consultation with her clinician.
