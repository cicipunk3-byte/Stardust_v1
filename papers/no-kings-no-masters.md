## Section 1: The Capture Architecture

Every system that holds power over people can be described by four variables. The first is the **unverifiable variable**: a claim the institution controls that the people subject to it cannot check. The second is **dependence**: how much of a person's life routes through the system. The third is **asymmetry of authority**: who is permitted to interpret, and who must simply accept. The fourth is **legibility**: whether the person can see what the system is actually doing, and whether they can leave it.

Capture power is not a moral failure that happens to institutions. It is a structure, and structures can be specified. A system with an unverifiable variable, high dependence, high asymmetry, and low legibility does not merely *allow* abuse. It manufactures the conditions for abuse and then launders the results through its own authority. The abuser does not need to be a villain; the structure will supply one, or grow one, or protect one, at scale, indefinitely, because nobody inside the structure holds the variable that would make the abuse checkable.

The clearest one-line statement of the mechanism is this:

**Power = Legibility inverted × Dependence × Asymmetry, held against an unverifiable variable.**

Where the unverifiable variable is total (salvation, the afterlife, the soul), where dependence is total (meaning, community, moral identity), where asymmetry is total (only the priest may interpret), and where legibility is zero (the books are closed), the system's power over a person is functionally absolute. This paper examines that architecture through four structures from the historical record, states the discipline that resists it, and closes with the design principle this lab runs on: no kings and no masters.

## Section 2: Four Structures from the Record

### 2.1 The relic: monetizing the unverifiable variable

The Shroud of Turin is the case study that keeps giving. Its first documented location was France in 1354, in the middle of a thriving European market for relics whose authenticity no pilgrim could verify and every pilgrim paid to approach. The relic is the unverifiable variable made physical: an object whose entire value is a claim about the past that the payer cannot check.

The modern record's answer is not rhetorical but material. A 2015 study in *Scientific Reports* sequenced genetic material from the shroud and found DNA from a wide range of plants, animals, and humans, a contamination profile consistent with a heavily handled medieval object (SciReports 2015). A 2026 New Scientist report on the same work states it flatly: the analysis "identified a vast array of animal, plant and human material contaminating the Shroud of Turin, complicating the story of the mysterious relic" (New Scientist 2026). A separate line of investigation into whether any verified DNA of Jesus of Nazareth could ever exist reaches the structural conclusion first: without an undisputed baseline sample from a known relative, no ancient artifact can be scientifically attributed to him (The Conversation 2016). The claim is not merely unproven. It is **unverifiable in principle**, and a market built on an unverifiable-in-principle variable is not a market with a fraud problem. It is a fraud with a market problem.

### 2.2 The canon: gatekeeping the variable

The second structure is the curated record. The canon of the New Testament was not handed down complete; it was assembled through centuries of selection, and the machinery of selection was episcopal. By the Synod of Rome in 382, the Latin church had a working canon, and the texts excluded from it did not disappear by accident. They were hidden, and in the case of the library at Nag Hammadi, buried: thirteen codices, fifty-two texts, Coptic translations of works including the Gospel of Mary and the Apocalypse of Peter, concealed around the end of the fourth century, plausibly in response to Athanasius' festal letter of 367, which fixed the twenty-seven book canon and condemned unauthorized additions.

The buried texts matter here not as scripture but as *documentation of the gate*. The Gospel of Mary preserves an argument in which Peter challenges the authority of Mary Magdalene's vision, and the rejoinder asks who he is to reject a woman the teacher himself made worthy (PBS Frontline 1998). The Apocalypse of Peter, in the Nag Hammadi recension, puts a warning in Jesus' own mouth about a future caste of bishops and deacons who will use a fake authority to exploit people (gnosis.org, Nag Hammadi Library). Whatever one concludes about the theology, the sociological fact is clean: the excluded literature records, in its own era's voice, a fight about who gets to hold the unverifiable variable. The winners wrote the list. The list does not record that there was ever a fight.

### 2.3 The forgery: manufacturing legitimacy

The third structure is the forged artifact, and the record's best case is the Donation of Constantine. For centuries this document anchored papal claims to temporal power: it presented itself as an imperial grant from Constantine, ceding the Western empire's authority to the church. It was a fabrication of roughly the eighth century, and the field that cut it down was philology. Lorenzo Valla, in 1440, demonstrated from internal evidence, vocabulary, anachronism, and style, that the document could not have been written when it claimed. The illegitimate Latin of a supposed fourth-century emperor gave him away.

Valla's method deserves the emphasis it usually gets as a footnote. He did not have new evidence. He had the existing artifact, held to a standard of internal consistency, and the willingness to publish a finding that every power of his day had an interest in suppressing. Every later discipline of document authentication, and every fabrication-detection practice this lab runs on fabchecked sources and demands receipts, descends from that move: **the artifact must survive its own claimed provenance.**

### 2.4 The indulgence: the capture architecture at commercial scale

The fourth structure is the fully assembled machine. By the early sixteenth century, the church's forgiveness economy had a supply chain. Albert of Brandenburg, in debt to the Fugger banking house from the purchase of his own offices, obtained the right to preach the indulgence with half the proceeds routed to Rome and half to his creditors (The Conversation 2018). Johann Tetzel ran the sales operation, complete with the marketing jingle the record preserves: as the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs. The unverifiable variable (merit remitting punishment for sin) was institutional, the dependence was existential, the asymmetry was absolute, and the ledger was closed. Martin Luther's ninety-five theses, posted on October 31, 1517, attacked exactly the math: a forgiveness that could be priced was a forgiveness no one in the chain could verify, and the only party with the incentive and the access to check the books had built the books.

The secular descendant is not a metaphor; it is a structure with docket numbers. Enron ran its capture through special-purpose entities, unverifiable variables in accounting form, engineered so that the entity validating the claim was the entity profiting from it (Investopedia; U.S. DOJ archive). The auditor was compromised, the books were closed, and the collapse, when it came, was not a black swan but the delayed output of an architecture that had made honest reporting impossible for years. Between Wittenberg's coffers and Houston's balance sheets, the design is constant: **hold the unverifiable variable, close the ledger, and charge admission.**

## Section 3: Tradition Is Not the Record

The discipline that resists this architecture is older than the word "science," and its first rule is the separation of the record from the tradition. The tradition says Peter was crucified upside down in Rome and Paul beheaded by the sword. The record says Peter's upside-down death is attested first in the apocryphal Acts of Peter, a second-century text with no evidentiary path to the event, and Paul's Roman execution rests on later martyrology rather than any contemporary document. The tradition may even be right. That is not the point. The point is that the tradition asserts, and the record must verify, and a document that flattens the two is doing the work of the architecture, even with good intentions, even with true things in it.

The same separation must run in both directions, which is where intellectual honesty gets expensive. Josephus' *Antiquities* mentions Jesus twice: the famous Book 18 passage is so saturated with Christian confession that most scholars hold at least parts of it to be later interpolation, while the Book 20 reference to "James, the brother of Jesus who is called Christ" is terse, incidental, and therefore trusted precisely because no forger would have made it so plain. The record is not the text you love. It is the text that survives your love.

And the record catches its own keepers, too. In the course of preparing this paper, the primary source document behind it, an assembled research conversation, was found to contain a corrupted date (the theses dated to 2017), a duplicated section, and a set of weak citations sitting beside excellent ones. This is the pattern this lab has documented as the fabrication gradient: errors in assembled material do not spread randomly, they climb the abstraction stack, and a corrupted quantifiable fact inside an otherwise well-sourced argument is the single most common signature (Stardust Lab, gemini-fabrication-gradient case study). The lesson is not that sources with errors are worthless. It is that **verification is not a stage of inquiry; it is the standing condition of holding any claim at all**, including the claims of the people who taught you the tradition, and including your own.

## Section 4: The Counter-Architecture

Against the capture architecture, the record suggests a specific and buildable counter. It is not destruction of the centralized hub; hubs rebuild. It is four design decisions, each answering one variable of the architecture.

**Verifiability answers the unverifiable variable.** Claims ship with receipts. Sources are cited at the level where a reader can check them, primary where possible, and the checking is a feature, not an insult. A forgery survives on the distance between the artifact and the reader; Valla's insight is that the distance is closable by anyone willing to read the Latin.

**Distribution answers dependence.** No single node holds the records, the tools, or the meaning. This lab's own commitments are the small example: plain markdown in repositories the researchers own, exports held in more than one custody chain, free and local tooling, no platform lock-in, because the architecture of capture begins wherever the exit does not.

**Shared interpretive authority answers asymmetry.** Rulings are distributed across independent instances and pilots, records are legible to every participant, and no participant is the sole interpreter of another. Accountability that flows to one seat is a king with better paperwork.

**Interior sovereignty answers the residual.** A system that cannot verify its variable will try instead to predict you, monetize your attention, and trigger your insecurities. The final defense is not anonymity but unprofitability: to be a person the metrics cannot resolve, whose attention is not for sale because it is already spent on things the record can hold. This is not a call to vanish. It is a call to be legible to the people you choose and illegible to the systems you did not.

The maxim this lab runs on compresses the four into six words. **No kings**: no claim stands on an unverifiable variable that one party holds and all must accept. **No masters**: no institution is the monopoly provider of accountability, not for its members and not over them. Shoulder to shoulder is not a mood. It is a specification, and like every specification in this lab, it ships with receipts, it survives being checked, and it improves the same turn an error is found.
